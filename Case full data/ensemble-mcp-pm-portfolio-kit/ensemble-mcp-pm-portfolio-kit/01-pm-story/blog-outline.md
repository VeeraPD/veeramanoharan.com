# Suggested portfolio blog structure

## Working title

**One builder, an AI coding team: how I built Ensemble to explore cheaper, parallel development**

## Opening

Use a concrete first-person hook: more than 50% lower API costs while building my iOS app, with parallel work finishing faster than solo runs. Label this as anecdotal immediately. Connect it to the product question: when does dividing work among models become worth the coordination?

## 1. The problem I wanted to solve

Premium models do both difficult decisions and routine implementation. Independent tasks often wait behind each other. Explain the audience and intended outcome without inventing customer research.

## 2. The product idea

A capable lead plans and reviews; cheaper workers build independent parts. Explain the benefits in plain language and show how the lead stays responsible for the whole result.

## 3. Why the opportunity could grow

One plan can support more implementation work. Independent tasks can run together. The benefit grows only if coordination, repeated context and rewrites do not grow as quickly. Emphasize task structure over project size alone.

## 4. What Ensemble does

Walk through a request: define shared rules, assign focused jobs and files, work in parallel, enforce write ownership, review and integrate. An example split could be storage, search and checks; use the to-do app roles when describing the actual benchmark.

## 5. The decisions behind the experience

Explain shared contracts, assigned files, selected context, configuration and a deliberately narrow tool surface. Discuss what was cut and why. State your role as a solo builder directing and reviewing AI-assisted development.

## 6. What I learned from the evidence

Use a small table: Sonnet solo vs team cost and time; Opus pilot separately. Explain the manager's share of cost. Give earlier worker timing its own scope. Cover the import-path lesson and common acceptance checks briefly.

## 7. Where I would take it next

A larger, clearly divided task; frozen acceptance criteria; comparable settings; repeated matched runs; cost and time including repairs. Invite readers to inspect or try the open-source tool.

## Figure plan

- Cover: anecdotal iOS experience, explicitly labeled.
- Figure 2: why shared planning and parallel work could help on larger well-divided tasks.
- Figure 3: Ensemble's lead/worker/review flow.
- Figure 4: measured small-study result and coordination tradeoff.

All four PNGs and the matching PDF are included. The blog should add narrative and reasoning rather than merely transcribe the carousel. A detailed invoice reconstruction or timing graph needs new source data; do not manufacture one.

## Voice

First person singular, clear, practical and optimistic. Explain benefits before mechanics. Pair each important number with what was measured. Avoid implying an audited 50% saving, universally faster delivery, a human development team or proven quality superiority.
