How this works (3 sentences)

Sharing goods earns 3 gold each per day, while mutual snatching traps both traders with just 1 measly coin each.

The Living Valley (Evolution)

What this shows: How prosperous characters inspire new apprentices while losing philosophies vanish from the valley.

How this works (3 sentences)

Under replicator dynamics, each generation trades across the valley and successful characters grow their share of the town. Any guild philosophy that falls below 0.1% of the valley loses its last workshop and goes extinct. Who survives depends on the starting mix of neighbors and whether foggy blunders break fragile alliances.

The Royal Curio Bazaar (Auction House)

Net Profit: 0 Gold

What this shows: How bazaar rules change your best bid and why winning a mystery pirate jar often loses gold.

How this works (3 sentences)

In a first-price auction, bidding your full value leaves zero profit, so clever collectors shade their bids downward. In a second-price auction, bidding your exact true value is always best because the price you pay is set by the second-highest rival. When bidding on the Sunken Pirate Jar where everyone only guesses the coin count, the winner usually had the wildest overestimate and suffers the winner's curse.

Meet Your Rival Collectors & Rules
1. First-Price Sealed Bid

If a curio is worth 80 gold to you and you bid 80, your profit when you win is zero. Against 3 rivals, shading your bid to 3/4 of your value (60 gold) balances your odds of winning with a healthy profit.

2. Second-Price (Vickrey) Sealed Bid

The winner pays only what the second-place rival bid. Overbidding risks buying at a loss, and underbidding misses profitable wins. Bidding your exact true value is always the dominant choice.

3. Sunken Pirate Jar & The Winner's Curse

The Barnacle Jar holds the same true coin count for everyone, and each collector squints through the glass to make a noisy guess. Bold Barnaby bids his full optimistic guess and usually overpays!
