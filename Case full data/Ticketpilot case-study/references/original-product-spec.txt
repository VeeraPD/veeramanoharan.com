TicketPilot: Product Spec

Portfolio project for the Celonis Senior Applied Value Engineer role · One-day build · Oct 2026

1. Summary

TicketPilot is an AI service desk assistant. It does three things:

Routes tickets to the team that will actually fix them, based on how similar tickets were really resolved.
Gives resolvers a suggested fix, with sources, from past tickets and help articles.
Alerts product teams to rising issues before they become crises, with a ready-to-act brief.

It accepts typed tickets and short voice notes in English, Hindi, or Tamil.

Headline claim to prove: Higher first-time-right routing than keyword rules, with measured hours saved.

2. The Problem
Ticket ping-pong. Tickets often go to the wrong team first. Each handoff adds delay, and staff waste time triaging work that isn’t theirs.
Slow fixes. Resolvers solve the same problems repeatedly because past fixes are buried in old tickets.
Product teams hear late. A broken release can generate dozens of tickets before anyone tells the team that shipped it.
Language barriers. Users who aren’t comfortable writing English file vague tickets, or don’t file at all.

Cost of doing nothing: Longer outages, wasted support hours, frustrated employees, and repeat issues that never get fixed at the source.

3. Goals and Non-Goals

Goals (targets for the demo dataset)

Goal	Target
Route tickets correctly the first time	80%+ (vs. keyword baseline)
Cut handoffs per ticket	50% fewer than baseline
Fix suggestions resolvers find useful	70%+ rated relevant in evaluation
Detect seeded issue spikes	100% of planted spikes caught within 1 day
Correctly route voice tickets	4 of 5 test clips

Non-Goals

Out of scope	Why
Connecting to a real helpdesk (ServiceNow, Jira)	Not needed to prove the concept; adds setup risk
Fine-tuning a model	Retrieval learns faster and costs less
Live phone calls	Recorded voice notes prove the point
Auto-closing tickets	A human should stay in control at this stage
Training simulator for staff	Separate product; distracts from the ROI story
4. Users and User Stories

Employee (ticket raiser)

As an employee, I want to describe my problem in my own words or language so I don’t have to guess the right category.
As an employee, I want to send a voice note in Hindi or Tamil so I can report issues quickly.

Resolver (support engineer)

As a resolver, I want only tickets my team can fix, so I stop wasting time passing work on.
As a resolver, I want a suggested fix with sources, so I can solve known problems faster and check the advice.

Support manager

As a manager, I want low-confidence tickets sent to a human, so the AI never guesses on hard cases.
As a manager, I want to see routing accuracy over time, so I know the system is improving.

Product manager

As a PM, I want an alert when tickets about my product spike, so I can fix the cause before more users are hit.
As a PM, I want issues ranked by impact, so I know what to prioritize.
5. How It Works

The two loops are what make it more than a router. Every closed ticket teaches the memory, and every cluster of closed tickets can trigger a product alert.

6. Requirements
P0: Must have (the demo fails without these)
#	Requirement	Acceptance criteria
P0-1	Ticket intake	Accepts free-text tickets. Extracts issue, urgency, and any deadline mentioned.
P0-2	Smart routing	Finds the 5 most similar past tickets, checks who closed them, and recommends a team with a confidence score and a one-line reason.
P0-3	Human fallback	Below the confidence threshold, the ticket goes to human review. The agent never auto-assigns a low-confidence ticket.
P0-4	Fix suggestions (RAG)	Shows suggested fix steps with links to the source tickets or help articles. If nothing relevant is found, it says so instead of inventing a fix.
P0-5	Continuous learning	Closed tickets are added to memory automatically. Reassignments are logged as corrections and used as examples for future routing.
P0-6	Product alerts	Groups tickets by meaning, flags any group that grows 3x above its normal rate, and generates a brief: issue, start date, impact, likely cause, suggested owner, priority.
P0-7	Baseline comparison	Keyword-rules router runs on the same test set. Results shown side by side.
P0-8	Voice tickets	Accepts short audio clips in English, Hindi, and Tamil. Transcribes, translates to English, and routes like a text ticket.
P1: Nice to have (if time allows)
#	Requirement	Acceptance criteria
P1-1	Knowledge gap drafts	When tickets are resolved with no matching help article, a draft article is created for human approval.
P1-2	Urgency correction	Flags tickets marked “low” that mention deadlines, outages, or many affected users.
P1-3	Accuracy trend chart	Weekly first-time-right routing rate, showing improvement as memory grows.
P1-4	Stress-test set	An agent generates 20 tricky tickets (vague, angry, two issues in one, mixed language) for evaluation.
P2: Future (documented, not built)
Screenshot reading for error messages
Duplicate ticket merging during outages
Spoken replies in the user’s language
Live integration with ServiceNow or Jira
Self-service fix suggestions before a ticket is filed
7. Voice Demo Spec

Model: faster-whisper, “small” size, running locally on CPU.

Why this model:

Lightweight. Runs on a normal laptop, no GPU, no paid API.
Wide language coverage. Whisper supports multilingual speech recognition across 99 languages, plus language identification, so the app detects the language automatically. 
uva
Simple to integrate. A few lines of Python. Built-in translate-to-English mode.

How the voice flow works:

User uploads a 10–30 second clip.
faster-whisper detects the language and produces a transcript.
Claude cleans up the transcript, translates it to English, and extracts the issue.
The ticket enters the normal routing flow.
The original transcript and detected language are stored with the ticket.

Demo clips (record yourself or ask friends):

Clip	Language	Content
1	English	VPN keeps disconnecting
2	Hindi	Can’t log into the payroll portal
3	Tamil	Laptop screen flickering
4	Hindi-English mix	Sales dashboard showing access denied
5	English, noisy background	Printer not working on 3rd floor

Known limits (state these openly in the README):

Mixed Hindi-English speech is the weakest case for the small model. Technical terms like “VPN” may get mangled.
Upgrade path: Indic-focused models do better here. For example, Indic-Transcribe-Flex covers 27 Indian languages and transcribes Hinglish as it’s actually spoken rather than forcing it into one language. It’s heavier, so it’s a next step, not a day-one choice. 
huggingface
Future spoken replies: Kokoro 82M is a lightweight open-weight text-to-speech model that supports 8 languages including Hindi. A good fit for P2. 
openrouter

Layer	Choice	Why
Language	Python 3.11+	Matches the JD; best AI library support
LLM	Claude API	Reasoning, extraction, translation, brief writing
Agent workflow	LangGraph	Handles branching and the human-approval pause cleanly

Topic grouping	Embeddings + HDBSCAN clustering	Groups tickets by meaning without setting the number of groups upfront

Tracing + evals	LangSmith	Step-by-step traces; supports your results section
Front end	Streamlit	A working UI in an hour
Data generation	Claude	Synthetic tickets with realistic patterns

Pin all package versions. LangChain changes often.

10. Evaluation and Success Metrics

Headline comparison (the core of your README):

Metric	Keyword baseline	TicketPilot
First-time-right routing	measure	measure
Average handoffs per ticket	measure	measure
Estimated hours saved per year	—	calculate

Other measures:

Fix suggestion relevance: Claude-as-judge scores each suggestion against the actual resolution notes. Spot-check 10 by hand to confirm the judge is fair.
Spike detection: Both planted spikes caught? How many days earlier than a human would notice?
Voice accuracy: Clips routed correctly out of 5.
Escalation rate: Percentage of tickets sent to human review. Too high means the system isn’t useful; too low means it’s overconfident.

Kill criteria (what would make you recommend stopping):

Routing accuracy beats the baseline by less than 10 points
Wrong auto-assignments exceed 5% of high-confidence tickets
Escalation rate exceeds 40%

Simple ROI formula for the CFO one-pager:

Hours saved = tickets per year × handoffs avoided per ticket × average delay per handoff
Add resolver time saved from fix suggestions. Subtract API and hosting costs.

11. Why This Beats Other Solutions
Alternative	Its weakness	What TicketPilot does differently
Keyword rules	Matches words, not meaning. “Access denied” goes to Security every time.	Understands the problem and checks what actually fixed similar tickets.
Category dropdowns	Users pick wrong or choose “Other.”	No categories needed. Users describe the problem in their own words or voice.
Classic ML classifiers	Learn from the first team assigned, which is often wrong. Need retraining to improve.	Learns from who resolved the ticket. Improves instantly as tickets close.
Generic chatbot / LLM alone	Knows nothing about how this company works. Gives generic answers.	Grounded in the company’s own ticket history and help articles, with sources.
Typical helpdesk AI add-ons	Usually focus on one job (routing or suggestions) and stay inside the support team.	Connects support to product teams. Turns ticket noise into prioritized fixes.
English-only systems	Exclude users who prefer their own language.	Accepts voice in Hindi, Tamil, and English.

The one-line pitch: Other tools guess based on what a ticket says. TicketPilot decides based on what actually happened, which is the same idea behind Celonis’s “AI needs process context” message.
