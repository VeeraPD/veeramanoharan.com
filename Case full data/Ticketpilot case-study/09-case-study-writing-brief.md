# Brief for writing the portfolio case study

## Core argument

I can connect process evidence, an AI intervention, a usable work experience and an executive investment decision. I can also say when the evidence does not support deployment.

The strongest outcome available today is the **quality of the decision and learning**, supported by a working prototype and transparent failed evaluation. It is not achieved customer ROI. Avoid making a polished product film the results section.

## Suggested narrative and exhibit order

| Section | Reader's question | Material / exhibit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | What problem, for whom, and what did you own? | Product brief; individual-project scope; trailer |
| Discovery | Why investigate this process? | 7,554 incidents / 9,454 handoffs / 15.6% bounce evidence, with observational limit |
| Hypotheses | What had to be true? | Avoidability, ownership predictability, source usefulness and workflow-fit assumptions |
| Priorities | What did you choose and cut? | Eight P0s to five; MoSCoW; non-AI alternatives; scope decisions |
| Product | How does it work in an organization? | One request; TicketPilot classifies, engineer approves, employee confirms, manager monitors |
| AI choices | Why this implementation? | Inspectable retrieval/rules, sourced fixes, confidence gate, implementation boundary |
| Measurement | What would success mean? | Durable first-owner resolution NSM; model/process/adoption/value metric tree |
| Evaluation | What happened when tested? | 10% lexical vs 90% keyword; 90% review; preserved failed gate |
| Iteration | What changed after feedback? | Product comprehension, role clarity, tutorial, longer film and concise trailer |
| Economics | Why would anyone fund the next step? | Conditional value/TCO and sensitivity to review effort |
| Recommendation | Expand, change or stop? | HOLD current-router deployment; bounded discovery; explicit pilot gates |
| Reflection | What would you do differently? | Real-text validation earlier, sharper eligibility, better metric/gate alignment, measured comprehension |

Use roughly 1,200-1,800 words for a main portfolio narrative, with expandable technical/evidence sections and media. This is a proposed editorial structure, not a required format. The executive opening should fit in a short paragraph and explain the decision before listing the stack.

## Claims that are defensible today

- I reproduced historical ownership patterns from a public incident log with documented definitions and provenance.
- I built an integrated local prototype and an inspectable classification/source workflow.
- I measured a failed synthetic baseline comparison and retained the case-level results.
- I narrowed scope and revised the user journey in response to direct project feedback.
- I proposed an NSM, experiment plan and conditional business case with explicit gates.
- I produced a trailer, detailed walkthrough and executive one-pagers.

## Claims that need new evidence

Customer interviews completed; validated market demand; willingness to pay; production adoption; reduced handoffs or resolution time; improved employee satisfaction; verified fix usefulness; positive real-text model lift; live enterprise integration; production LLM agents; measured customer ROI; a warning-free security/production audit.

Do not say the lexical model beat the keyword baseline, that 0/5 proves safe automation, or that 64.9 elapsed hours are labor saved. Do not describe the original proposed agent stack as implemented. Do not present sampled/seeded dashboard numbers as measured product analytics.

## Role-specific framing

For AI-PM: emphasize user/problem hypotheses, feature cuts, measurement, AI quality/coverage tradeoffs and changes after feedback. For FDE: show the integrated workflow and the enterprise boundaries still to solve. For Celonis-oriented senior value engineering: lead with process context, demonstrate operational fit, preserve the failed gate, and make the investment recommendation concrete.

The Celonis role informed the project brief. This case does not claim use of the Celonis platform, customer access, affiliation or endorsement. Confirm the current posting before tailoring an application; the reference draft preserves the role link consulted earlier.

## Authorship and collaboration statement

Use first person for work the portfolio owner can explain and defend. State that this was an individual portfolio project supported by AI development tools. Do not invent teammates, engineering sprints, customer workshops or steering-committee approvals. Personas and the Northstar organization are fictional. Research and pilot owners are proposed roles.

## Questions to resolve with the portfolio owner

1. What prior work or personal observation motivated the initial hypothesis? Include only genuine context.
2. Which choices can the owner explain without reading the code? Prepare concrete tradeoff examples.
3. Is there any actual external interview, recruiter feedback or usability session that can be documented with permission?
4. What was the actual project effort and sequence? The one-day original plan is not a time log.
5. Is the next case primarily AI-PM, FDE or value engineering? Keep one main narrative and adapt the opening.
6. What real next experiment is feasible, and what evidence would change HOLD to pilot?
7. Will the hosted integrated demo be available when the case is published? Use the videos as a fallback.

## Before writing a results headline

Check every number against [the evidence ledger](evidence/README.md). Separate observed, offline, sample, scenario and proposed statements. Resolve gate inconsistencies in the next experimental contract; preserve the current failed result. Use a dated source snapshot and describe later changes as new evidence rather than silently rewriting the past.
