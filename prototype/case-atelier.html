Atélier: search your handwritten notes by meaning

MBA students write their notes by hand on iPad, but search only finds a page if they remember the exact words they wrote.

Problem

Students remember the idea, not the words, so their notes go unfound

At IIM Lucknow most of my cohort writes notes by hand on an iPad. Classes move fast, quizzes come often and the notes pile up. I started by talking to classmates before quizzes and case discussions. Three frustrations came up each time.

  • Search fails on memory. People remember the idea from class, not the exact words they wrote, so keyword search comes back empty.
  • Review is manual. Preparing for a quiz means scrolling notebook by notebook, page by page.
  • The workaround costs thinking. Typing makes search easy but loses the sketches, arrows and structure that come with writing by hand.

Why now: handwriting recognition on iPad became good enough to trust, and language models made it practical to search a person's own notes by meaning.

When I tried the three handwriting apps in July 2026, search in each matched the words on the page, not their meaning. That is the open space Atélier goes after.

Strategy

The wedge is exam-season review; incumbents can copy search, so the review workflow has to be the moat

  • Wedge: grad students under heavy review pressure. They feel the pain weekly and I can test with them directly.
  • Why it could hold: summaries grounded only in your own pages, and echoes that connect today's note to older ones, make the product more useful the more you write. This is a hypothesis, not yet shown.
  • Risk: Apple Notes or GoodNotes could add search by meaning. If they do, search alone stops being the difference, and review is what is left.

Key insight

Key insight

People remember the idea, not the words.

A student searching for "price discrimination" may have written "charging different customers differently". So search had to work on meaning, and summaries had to stay inside the user's own notes.

Hypotheses

Three bets, with targets written down before building

  • H1. Meaning beats keywords. Search by meaning puts the right note in the top 3 for most real student queries. Target: 16 of 20.
  • H2. Summaries earn a place in review. Students use a summary at least once in a week with a quiz.
  • H3. Writing feel is the price of entry. If the pen feels worse than their current app, they never stay long enough to try search.

Decisions and trade-offs

Three calls: pen first, summaries grounded in your notes, only two AI features

01Writing experience first

What I chose Chosen

A solid pen, eraser, colour, lasso and ruler toolkit before any AI

What I gave up

Weeks of delay before the riskiest bet, search by meaning, could be tested

Why. If writing feels bad, nobody stays long enough to need search. In hindsight I would have tested search on classmates' existing notes first.

02Grounded summaries

What I chose Chosen

Summaries draw only from the user's own notes

What I gave up

"Smarter" summaries that add outside knowledge

Why. Students need the summary to reflect their class, not the internet. The eval checks whether it does.

03Scope

What I chose Chosen

Search and summaries only; no chat assistant or auto-flashcards in v1

What I gave up

Feature-list appeal

Why. Fewer AI features, each one tested properly.
