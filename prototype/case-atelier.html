Atélier: an AI notes app for iPad

Handwriting-first notes you can search by meaning and summarise in one tap. I took it from a problem I lived as an MBA student to a feature-complete iPad app, solo.

Skip to the decisions

Problem

Students write pages of notes on iPad and cannot find them again. Keyword search fails when you do not remember the exact words you wrote.

What I did

Ran discovery with classmates, bet on search by meaning, cut v1 to two AI features, and designed and built the app in Swift.

Status

Context

Why this, why now

At IIM Lucknow most of my cohort takes notes by hand on an iPad. Classes move fast, quizzes come often, and the notes pile up across subjects and weeks.

Two things changed recently. Handwriting recognition on iPad became good enough to trust, and language models made it practical to search a person's own notes by meaning, not just by matching words. That opened a gap: handwritten notes are still treated as pictures you scroll, not knowledge you can ask.

Problem

Pages of notes, never found again

In conversations with classmates before quizzes and case discussions, the same three frustrations came up.

  • Search fails on memory. People remember the idea from class, not the exact words they wrote, so keyword search comes back empty.
  • Review is manual. Preparing for a quiz means scrolling notebook by notebook, page by page.
  • The workaround costs thinking. Switching to typed notes makes search easy but loses the sketches, arrows and structure that come with writing by hand.

What I found when I tried these apps in July 2026: handwriting search in all three matched the words on the page, not their meaning. Atélier tests whether search by meaning on handwritten notes is worth building.

Key insight

Key insight

People remember the idea, not the words.

A student searching for "price discrimination" may have written "charging different customers differently". So search had to work on meaning, not exact keywords. That one insight shaped the product: search by meaning, and summaries grounded only in the user's own notes.

Hypotheses

The bets, written down before building

  • H1. Meaning beats keywords. Search by meaning finds the right note in the top 3 for most real student queries. Target: 16 of 20.
  • H2. Summaries earn a place in review. Students use a summary at least once in a week with a quiz.
  • H3. Writing feel is the price of entry. If the pen feels worse than their current app, students never stay long enough to try search.

Solution

Decisions and trade-offs

Three calls, and what each one cost

01Writing experience first

What I chose Chosen

Built a solid pen, eraser, colour, lasso and ruler toolkit before any AI

What I gave up

Weeks of delay before the riskiest bet, search by meaning, could be tested

Why. If writing feels bad, nobody stays long enough to need search. In hindsight, I would have tested search on classmates' existing notes first (see What I learned).

02Grounded summaries

What I chose Chosen

Summaries draw only from the user's notes

What I gave up

"Smarter" summaries that add outside knowledge

Why. Students need the summary to reflect their class, not the internet. The eval checks whether it does.

03Scope

What I chose Chosen

Search and summaries only. No chat assistant, no auto-flashcards in v1

What I gave up

Feature-list appeal

Why. Fewer AI features, each one tested properly.

What I learned

Test the riskiest bet first

  • I built the writing core before testing search by meaning, the bet the whole product rests on. A cheaper order: run search on classmates' existing notes first, then build the notebook only if search works.
  • Writing the targets down before the eval keeps the result honest, whatever it turns out to be.
