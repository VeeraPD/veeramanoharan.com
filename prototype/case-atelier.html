Atélier: search your handwritten notes by meaning

Problem statement

Find handwritten notes by meaning, even when you forget the exact words.

Problem

The right note is hard to find

Students accumulate handwritten notes across classes and notebooks. Before a quiz, they need to retrieve an idea quickly without remembering where they wrote it.

Issue

Memory works differently from keywords

A student remembers the concept, not necessarily the exact phrase on the page. Finding it can mean scrolling through notebooks instead of reviewing the material.

Solution

Search handwriting by meaning

Atélier combines an iPad writing experience with semantic search and summaries grounded in the user’s own notes. Ask in your own words, find the relevant page and get the gist.

Progress Built and demoed. Search evaluation and a classmate beta are next.

Problem

Students remember concepts, but keyword search needs exact words

Classmates at IIM Lucknow described three recurring frustrations before quizzes.

  • They remember concepts, not exact keywords.
  • Review means scrolling through notebooks.
  • Typing sacrifices the sketches and structure of handwriting.

Competition

My initial app comparisons revealed a concept-retrieval gap

When I tried the three handwriting apps in July 2026, search in each matched the words on the page, not their meaning. That is the open space Atélier goes after.

Strategy

Exam review gives semantic search a focused starting point

  • Start: graduate students preparing for quizzes.
  • Value: search and summaries grounded in their own notes.
  • Risk: competitors can add semantic search; the review experience must earn repeat use.

Hypotheses

Search quality and repeat use determine whether the product works

  • H1. Meaning beats keywords. Search by meaning puts the right note in the top 3 for most real student queries. Target: 16 of 20.
  • H2. Summaries earn a place in review. Students use a summary at least once in a week with a quiz.
  • H3. Writing feel is the price of entry. If the pen feels worse than their current app, they never stay long enough to try search.

Decisions and trade-offs

I prioritized writing quality and kept summaries grounded in notes

01Writing experience first

A solid pen, eraser, colour, lasso and ruler toolkit before any AI

Why: If writing feels bad, nobody stays long enough to need search. In hindsight I would have tested search on classmates' existing notes first.

Trade-off: Weeks of delay before the riskiest bet, search by meaning, could be tested

02Grounded summaries

Summaries draw only from the user's own notes

Why: Students need the summary to reflect their class, not the internet. The eval checks whether it does.

Trade-off: "Smarter" summaries that add outside knowledge

03Scope

Search and summaries only; no chat assistant or auto-flashcards in v1

Why: Fewer AI features, each one tested properly.

Trade-off: Feature-list appeal
