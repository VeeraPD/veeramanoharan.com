Multi-agent router

Can low-cost models do most of the work if a frontier model only plans and reviews? A personal tool, and an experiment in trading cost against quality.

Problem

One model for everything is expensive

I was using a frontier model for every step of my AI work. Most of those steps did not need it.

  • Drafting boilerplate code, reformatting text and summarising files are routine tasks.
  • Planning, judging quality and fixing hard bugs need the strongest model.
  • Paying top price for both meant costs grew with every project.

How it works

Plan at the top, work at the bottom

  • Layer 1, Director (Claude): owns the goal, splits it into streams, gives the final sign-off.
  • Layer 2, Leads: one per stream. Each breaks its stream into steps and reviews the result.
  • Layer 3, Workers: low-cost models with one job each, run in parallel.
  • Every output is reviewed. A failed step gets one retry, then moves up a layer.

Key insight

Key insight

The cheap model doesn't need to be right every time. The system does.

A low-cost worker that is right most of the time, inside an org that reviews every output and escalates misses, can match a frontier-only setup at a fraction of the cost. The structure, and especially the reviewer, is the product.

Goals and metrics

Cost, quality and time, as one decision

North StarCost per accepted taskNorth Star. Cheap output that fails review is not a saving.
Quality pass rateShare of tasks that pass a simple rubric
Time per taskWall-clock time, with parallel steps
Escalation rateShare of steps that end up back with Claude

Guardrail: pass rate no more than 5 points below the Claude-only baseline. That is the trade I accept for at least 40% lower cost: a failed step is caught in review and retried, so a small quality drop costs little. Past 5 points, rework eats the savings.

Where it breaks

Known failure modes

  • Data privacy: sending work to some third-party providers may break company policy. Fix: an approved-provider list per team.
  • Reviewer cost: if Claude has to redo too much, savings disappear. Watch the escalation rate.
  • Silent errors: a confident wrong answer can pass review. Fix: stricter checks on high-risk tasks, such as code that touches data.

What I learned

Evaluation is the real work

  • Routing logic is easy to write and hard to trust without a benchmark.
